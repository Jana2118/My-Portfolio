01 · Overview

Project overview

Introduction

When an accident happens, the person reporting it may be hurt, shaken or in a hurry. Every step between detection and help is a step they might not manage.

I designed the reporting experience around one emergency screen.

My role

UI/UX Designer, from October 2023 to March 2024.

02 · The challenge

The challenge

An emergency flow can’t rely on someone reading carefully, typing an address or finding a small button. It has to do as much as possible on its own and leave one obvious thing to press.

What made it hard

  • Stress

    People under stress can’t weigh many choices.

  • Location matters most

    Help needs to know where to go, and people often can’t say exactly where they are.

  • False alarms happen

    Detection can be wrong, so cancelling must be easy and calm.

03 · Goals

What it had to do

For the business

  • Fewer steps from detection to report
  • Reports that always include a location

For the people using it

  • Get help with one press
  • Never have to type where they are
  • Cancel easily if they’re fine

04 · The solution

The solution

The flow is one screen with three decisions, numbered on the recreation below.

01. Location, found for you

The screen shows where the person is.

What’s in the design

  • Location detected automatically and shown on a map.

Why it matters

Nobody has to describe where they are at the worst possible moment.

02. One big action

There is one main thing to do.

What’s in the design

  • One large primary button: Send alert.

Why it matters

A big target is easy to hit, even with shaking hands.

03. A quiet way out

Cancelling is there, but secondary.

What’s in the design

  • A smaller “I'm okay, cancel” option.

Why it matters

False alarms are handled without competing with the main action.
