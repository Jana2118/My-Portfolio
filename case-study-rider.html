01 · Overview

Project overview

Introduction

Selorg runs grocery deliveries from dark stores. Customers order, pickers pack, handheld scanners verify every item, and then a rider takes it the last mile. Selorg Rider is the app those delivery partners use for their whole shift.

It is one of the connected apps in the Selorg platform; the platform case study covers the others.

My role

As UX/UI Executive, I designed the second version of the rider app prototype, from sign-in to payouts.

02 · The challenge

The challenge

Riders use the app on the move, one-handed, between pickups and drops. They need to know what to do next, what each order pays, and what they have earned, without digging through menus. Their pay mixes per-order earnings, bulk deliveries and incentives, and some orders are cash on delivery, so the money side has to be crystal clear.

What made it hard

  • On the move

    Glanceable screens and big actions, for use between stops.

  • Mixed work

    Single orders and multi-drop bulk batches in the same shift.

  • Cash in hand

    Cash on delivery means riders carry money that has to be tracked and deposited.

  • Pay that adds up

    Per-order pay, bulk deliveries and incentives all count towards one payout.

03 · Goals

What it had to do

For the business

  • Assign single orders and bulk batches to riders who are online
  • Track cash collected on delivery
  • Motivate on-time delivery with clear incentives

For riders

  • Know at a glance whether they are online and what to do next
  • See what an order pays before accepting it
  • Understand every rupee of their payout

04 · The solution

The solution

Five tabs cover a shift from start to payout. Names, amounts and addresses in the screens are sample data.

01. Sign in, your way

A quick start: choose mobile, WhatsApp or email, and confirm with a one-time code.

What’s in the design

  • Three sign-in methods with a one-time code
  • A separate path to create a rider account
  • A designed wrong-code path, not only the happy path

Why it matters

Offering WhatsApp and email alongside SMS gives a rider another way in when one channel fails, right at the start of a shift.

02. Home: one switch and today at a glance

Going online is a single toggle; underneath, today’s numbers and the incentive show how the shift is going.

What’s in the design

  • A clear online/offline toggle, with the rider’s vehicle and delivery mode
  • Today’s performance: cash collected, orders delivered, online hours and slots completed
  • A daily incentive with a progress bar and the on-time/late count
  • Book more slots for upcoming shifts

Why it matters

The most important question a rider has, “am I receiving orders?”, is answered by the first thing on screen; the rest shows whether today’s target is in reach.

03. Orders and bulk batches

Live orders list what’s available to accept, with what each one pays, where to pick up and where to drop.

What’s in the design

  • A bulk batch summary: delivered, remaining and failed
  • Each order shows estimated payout, pickup and drop, distance, time and item count before accepting
  • Priority tags on urgent orders
  • History filtered by standard and bulk deliveries

Why it matters

Showing payout, distance and time before accepting lets riders make a fair choice quickly, and the batch summary keeps a long bulk run from feeling endless.

04. Earnings and profile

Riders see the week’s pay, when it arrives and how it was made, and manage their documents and cash deposits.

What’s in the design

  • This week’s earnings with deliveries, average per order and online hours
  • Next payout date and method, with breakdowns by type and by day
  • Profile with trips, on-time rate and float cash in hand
  • Documents and KYC, my shifts, floating cash and deposits, and help

Why it matters

Pay is the reason riders open the app. Breaking the total into standard, bulk and incentive earnings, day by day, means no payout comes as a surprise.

05 · Design details

Details that matter

  • Thumb-first layout

    Primary actions sit low and wide: accept order, verify and continue, the bottom tabs.

  • Cash made visible

    Cash on delivery and float cash appear on Home and Profile, with a place to record deposits.

  • Same platform, same language

    Orders, batches and dark stores use the same names as the admin console and picker app.

06 · Next steps

Where it goes next

  1. 01

    Ride along

    Test the app with riders on real routes, one-handed and outdoors.

  2. 02

    Measure acceptance

    Track how quickly orders are accepted once they appear.

  3. 03

    Close the cash loop

    Make depositing collected cash as quick as accepting an order.
