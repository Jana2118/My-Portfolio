01 · Overview

Project overview

Introduction

An organic grocery and delivery business run from dark stores. Four connected apps share one backend: customers order, pickers pack, handheld scanners verify every item, and an admin console runs the warehouse, stores and riders.

The admin console sums up the job in its own words: one console for warehouse, dark stores, pickers and riders, from central warehouse receiving through to the customer’s doorstep.

My role

I work on this platform as a UX Executive at Selorg Tech.

02 · The challenge

The challenge

One order passes through four sets of hands: the customer who places it, the picker who packs it, the handheld scanner that checks it, and the operations team that keeps stores, stock and riders running. Each of them needs a tool built for their situation, and all four need to agree on the same order.

What made it hard

  • Very different users

    Shoppers on their phones, pickers on the floor, and operations staff across eight roles.

  • Speed with accuracy

    Dark stores depend on fast picking, and every item is verified before it leaves.

  • Tough floor conditions

    The scanner is used with gloves on and with unreliable signal.

  • Identity and money

    Pickers are onboarded with ID checks and paid through the app, so trust has to be built in.

03 · Goals

What it had to do

For the business

  • Run warehouse, dark stores, pickers and riders from one console
  • Catch stalled picks and delayed transfers early
  • Verify every item before an order leaves

For the people using it

  • Shoppers: order fresh groceries quickly, as a guest or with a one-time code
  • Pickers: know their shift, attendance and earnings
  • On the floor: scan fast, even offline

04 · The solution

The solution

The platform is four apps that share one backend. Each one below shows screens from its prototype.

01. Admin dashboard

A role-based command centre for warehouse, dark stores, pickers and riders. Eight roles, from Super Admin to Catalog Manager, each see only their scope, behind two-factor sign-in and an audit log.

What’s in the design

  • Live order flow, SLA tracking and an alert feed for stalled picks and delayed transfers
  • Store and warehouse inventory, goods requests, receiving, putaway and transfer approvals
  • Rider and picker approvals, rosters, earning rules and payout runs
  • Catalog, promotions, home-page builder and content pipeline

Why it matters

Scoping each of the eight roles keeps every screen focused, and stops a mistake in one area from spilling into another. Two-factor sign-in and the audit log make every change traceable.

02. Customer shopping app

A mobile shopping app for fresh produce, millets, dairy and groceries. Customers sign in with a phone, WhatsApp or email code, or browse as a guest.

What’s in the design

  • Category browsing, search, offers and best-deal shelves
  • Cart with coupons and tip; pay by wallet, UPI, card or net banking
  • Order tracking from confirmed to delivered, plus cancellations, returns and refunds
  • Rider chat, support tickets and saved addresses, with loading, empty and error states designed

Why it matters

Guest browsing and one-time codes remove the account wall before people have seen a single product. Designed loading, empty and error states mean the app still explains itself when the network or the stock doesn’t cooperate.

03. Picker workforce app

A workforce app for warehouse and dark-store pickers, covering the full journey from application to payout.

What’s in the design

  • Onboarding with Aadhaar and PAN checks, face verification and bank details
  • Shift selection, device collection with a manager code, and location-checked check-in
  • Attendance, overtime, weekly earnings and incentive tracking
  • Clear states for under review, approved, rejected, blocked and suspended accounts

Why it matters

Pickers handle stock and get paid through the app, so identity, device and bank checks happen once, up front. Clear account states tell people exactly where they stand instead of leaving them guessing.

04. Handheld scanner app

A handheld scanner flow for the pick floor, built for speed with gloves on and unreliable signal.

What’s in the design

  • Scan the bag QR, then follow the pick list item by item
  • Scan outcomes for good, invalid, duplicate and rejected items, with a not-found path
  • Bag photo check and delivery-rack scan to close each order
  • Offline queue with sync retry, plus low-battery and session-expiry handling

Why it matters

On the pick floor, gloves and dropped signal are normal. Big, unmistakable scan outcomes and an offline queue that syncs later keep picking moving instead of stopping at the first error.

05 · Design details

Details that matter

  • One order, four views

    The same order appears in each app in the form that person needs: a cart for the shopper, a pick list for the picker, a scan checklist on the handheld, and a live row in the admin order flow.

  • Every state designed

    Loading, empty and error states in the customer app; under-review to suspended states for pickers; scan outcomes, offline and low-battery states on the handheld.

  • Access by role

    Eight admin roles with their own scope, two-factor sign-in and an audit log behind every change.

06 · Next steps

Where it goes next

  1. 01

    Measure the floor

    Track time per pick and scan errors per order to see where the floor slows down.

  2. 02

    Field-test the scanner

    Run sessions with pickers in gloves, in a working dark store.

  3. 03

    Close the loop for shoppers

    Ask for quick feedback after delivery and feed it back to stores.
