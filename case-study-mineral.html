01 · Overview

Project overview

Introduction

Mineral Bridge is a marketplace for buying and selling minerals, from gold doré bars and copper to lithium and gemstones. Its promise, in the product’s own words: buy and sell minerals, verified at every step. Lots are inspected, prices are agreed through quotations, trades are signed as e-contracts and deliveries are tracked, for buyers and sellers on the web and on mobile.

The people on it could hardly be more different: a solo artisanal miner selling a few grams, a trader moving kilograms, and refineries and manufacturers buying at scale.

My role

As UX/UI Executive, I worked across the web app, the mobile app and the documents that keep them in step: a screen-by-screen flow walkthrough of the web app, a gap analysis between the mobile and web flows, a user-flow and design specification for the next round of features, and a design brief for the public website.

02 · The challenge

The challenge

A mineral trade involves many hands: a seller, a buyer, an inspection and testing partner, a logistics partner and the platform itself. Every step has to be verified, and every party has to know what happens next. Reviewing the working prototypes, not just their screenshots, showed where the experience didn’t yet keep that promise.

What the review found

  • Small miners, big forms

    An artisanal miner who was ready to sell was sent into the same heavy institutional listing form as a trading company: permit number, target institutional buyer, compliance checkboxes.

  • No idea who the buyer is

    Any signed-in user could buy. The only classification was a single buyer-category dropdown buried inside the quantity screen.

  • Three ways to sell

    Mobile and web showed three slightly different selling experiences: the institutional web form, the artisanal-miner registration and a simpler mobile flow.

  • Missing pieces

    There was no auctions module anywhere in the build, and the training videos were reachable only through the miners’ dashboard.

04 · Goals

What it had to do

For the business

  • One set of rules for web and mobile: only layout and density differ
  • Verify every lot, every party and every payment
  • Add auctions without them looking bolted on

For the people using it

  • Buyers: find verified lots and always know the next step
  • Small miners: list a mineral in under a minute once their profile exists
  • Everyone: learn the trade through short videos

05 · The solution

The solution

One marketplace on two surfaces. The screens below come from the working web and mobile prototypes; the names, prices and quantities in them are sample data.

01. Start by saying who you are

Right after the one-time code, the app asks how you will use Mineral Bridge: buyer, seller or both. Every later form then asks only for what that kind of account needs.

What’s in the design

  • Three choices as large cards: buyer, seller, or both from one account
  • Identity checks in one place: ID type, front and back, a selfie holding the ID, proof of address and a business document
  • Proposed tiers so a solo miner gives a few minutes of details, while a refinery completes full business checks

Why it matters

Asking once, up front, stops the platform from re-asking the same questions inside every buy and sell form, and it is what lets a small miner skip the institutional paperwork.

02. Buying verified lots

Buyers browse by category, drill into a lot and see its purity, availability and test reports before they commit.

What’s in the design

  • Five categories: precious, base, critical and industrial minerals, and gemstones
  • Forms within each mineral, such as doré bars, bullion, biscuits, dust, concentrate and grain
  • Every lot shows purity, availability, the testing company, the certificate number and its test reports

Why it matters

In a high-value trade, proof comes before price. Putting the test report and certificate on the lot itself lets a buyer judge quality before starting a quotation.

03. From quotation to delivery

Once a buyer requests a lot, the order moves through clear stages, and each one says whose turn it is.

What’s in the design

  • A progress list: order review, price quotation, e-contract, payment, logistics, delivery and completion
  • “Your turn” banners so the next action is never hidden
  • An e-contract that spells out quality, payment, delivery and how disputes are handled
  • Orders grouped into buying, selling and auctions, as cards or a table

Why it matters

A mineral trade passes through several partners and many steps. Showing the current stage and who acts next means nobody has to ask where an order stands.

04. Selling, and proving what you sell

Sellers pick the mineral they want to sell; inspection reports then follow the lot through to a seller confirmation.

What’s in the design

  • The same mineral list on web and mobile
  • Inspection reports the seller can accept, or question with a clarification request
  • Proposed: a quick listing for small miners (photo, mineral, quantity, unit), keeping the full form for institutional sellers

Why it matters

A seller who can see and respond to the inspection result takes part in the verification instead of waiting on it, and a lighter path for small lots answers the review’s first finding: small miners facing institutional paperwork.

05. Auctions

Time-boxed bidding on inspected lots, for sellers who want market pricing instead of a fixed offer. It was new: nothing like it existed in the earlier build.

What’s in the design

  • Live, upcoming and ended auctions, plus my bids
  • The current bid and a countdown on every lot
  • Bids rise in fixed steps, with quick-step buttons and an auto-bid option
  • The highest bid when the timer ends wins; the winner then pays to release the lot

Why it matters

Every part of the auction reuses patterns the app already had, so it feels like part of the product, and the rules are written on the screen, not left for bidders to guess.

06 · Design details

Details that matter

  • Browse first, verify when it counts

    Everything can be browsed without an account. Sign-in and then KYC are checked only at the moment someone buys, lists or bids, and after signing in they return to the same screen.

  • One logic, two layouts

    Content and field rules stay identical on web and mobile. The web uses a sidebar and a sticky summary panel; mobile uses a bottom bar, bottom sheets and a review screen.

  • Every step has an owner

    The prototype labels the steps run by the platform, the inspection partner or the logistics partner, so it is always clear who acts next.
