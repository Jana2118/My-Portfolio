01 · Overview

Project overview

Introduction

Sharing data safely comes down to a few questions: who is asking, for which data, with what access, and for how long.

This blockchain-based system lets people share data with permission, and gives them a dashboard to manage who can see what.

My role

UI/UX Designer, from May to June 2024.

02 · The challenge

The challenge

Permissions are easy to get wrong and hard to undo when people don’t understand them. Before anyone trusts a system with their data, they need to know what Read and Write mean, what happens when access expires, and how to take access back.

What made it hard

  • Two kinds of access

    Read and Write carry very different risk, and the difference has to be unmistakable.

  • Time-limited by default

    Access that never ends is access nobody remembers granting.

  • Reversible

    Taking access back has to be as easy as giving it.

  • Trust you can see

    The blockchain record behind the dashboard should show up as a signal, not stay hidden.

03 · Goals

What it had to do

For the business

  • Faster onboarding for new users
  • Fewer permission mistakes
  • Make the verified record a visible trust signal

For the people using it

  • Understand a request before approving it
  • Control how long access lasts
  • Take access back in one step

04 · The solution

The solution

The dashboard is a list of access requests with four decisions, numbered on the recreation below.

01. Access you can read at a glance

Each request shows its access type as a chip.

What’s in the design

  • Access type shown as a chip: Read or Write.

Why it matters

Write access gets the same visual weight as its risk, so it never slips through unnoticed.

02. Every grant expires

Each grant has an expiry.

What’s in the design

  • An expiry on every grant.

Why it matters

Access ends on its own unless someone chooses to renew it.

03. Two clear actions

Each row offers exactly one decision.

What’s in the design

  • Approve and Revoke as the only row actions.

Why it matters

The decision is always obvious, and always reversible.

04. Trust made visible

The dashboard shows the state of the record behind it.

What’s in the design

  • A visible chain-verified status.

Why it matters

People can see that what they’re looking at has been checked, without needing to understand how.

Try it yourself

Approve a request, choose how long access lasts, then revoke it again.

Where it goes next

    Explain on first use

    Remind before expiry

    Show who accessed what and when, for people who want to check.
