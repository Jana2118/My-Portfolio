01 · Overview

Project overview

Introduction

ROTA is Regami Solutions’ platform for over-the-air (OTA) updates to IoT devices. Teams discover devices, upload releases and create deployments from one dashboard with role-based access. A client on each device checks for updates and installs them with fail-safe modules.

Around updates, the platform covers the rest of a fleet’s life: configuring devices, monitoring their health and maintaining them remotely.

My role

UX Designer at Regami Solutions, from August 2024 to February 2025.

02 · The challenge

The challenge

A rollout is a long-running job spread across many devices. Each device can be waiting, updating, finished or failed. When that state is hard to read, the person in charge can’t tell whether to wait, step in or try again, and updates get left unfinished.

IoT fleets make it harder: devices run different hardware and operating systems, and connectivity in the field is often unstable.

What made it hard

  • Many devices, one view

    The dashboard has to summarise the whole fleet without hiding the devices that are failing.

  • Long-running work

    Updates take time. If progress isn’t visible, people abandon or restart them.

  • Failure is normal

    Some devices fail on the first try, so the cause and the fix have to be close at hand.

  • Mixed hardware, unstable networks

    Devices differ in hardware and operating system, and many sit on weak connections.

03 · Goals

What it had to do

For the business

  • Fewer deployments abandoned part-way
  • More updates completing successfully
  • One dashboard for the whole update lifecycle

For the people using it

  • Find the devices that need an update
  • Know which release is going out, and whether it’s approved
  • See a deployment’s progress and failures as it runs
  • Fix a failing device without a site visit

04 · The solution

The solution

The dashboard follows the update journey: find the devices, prepare the release, deploy it and watch it land, then look after each device. Each step below shows the real ROTA screen.

01. Discover devices

The update starts with knowing which devices you have.

What’s in the design

  • Filters to find the devices that need an update
  • Add a device and get a clear confirmation that it connected
  • Devices organised in a list and in device groups

Why it matters

A rollout is only as good as its target list. Filters and grouping make it quick to choose exactly the devices a release is for.

02. Prepare the release

Every build is a release with a clear identity and state.

What’s in the design

  • Each release shows its name, ID and version
  • A tag for its type: release or patch
  • An approval status on every card, such as In Draft or Approved
  • Code and deployment artifacts managed in one place

Why it matters

Seeing versions and approval states side by side makes it obvious which build is ready to go out, and which isn’t.

03. Deploy and watch it land

A deployment shows its progress while it runs.

What’s in the design

  • A progress bar split into successful, failed and in-progress devices
  • Counts of devices in the deployment, compatible, successful and failed
  • Success, overall and failure rates
  • Activity: who created, approved and deployed it, and when

Why it matters

Seeing success and failure as the rollout happens tells people whether to wait or step in, which is exactly the moment updates used to get abandoned.

04. Maintain each device

Every device has its own page for health and troubleshooting.

What’s in the design

  • Live status, such as Active or Downloading
  • Device ID, firmware version and network addresses
  • CPU, memory, temperature and storage at a glance
  • Connectivity, remote terminal access, and Reboot and Delete actions

Why it matters

When an update fails, the fix starts on the device. Health, logs and a terminal in one place mean remote troubleshooting instead of a site visit.
